The acceptance gate.

Six AI compute contracts filed with the SEC, worth about $35.1 billion in stated or maximum value, begin their recurring fees or rent on one of three events: a customer's acceptance of delivered hardware, a provider's ready notice, or a lease's commencement. Some also take prepayments earlier. Filings sometimes report a passed gate; IREN's annual report states that Microsoft accepted the first of four tranches in August 2026. No filing read for this page contains test results or an acceptance record behind such a statement.

Stated value behind a gate

Six customer contracts whose recurring fees or rent start on acceptance, a ready notice or commencement. $27.6B of it starts on the customer's own acceptance. The figure is full-term contract value as filed, not cash owed today.

4 of 10
Silence counts as acceptance

In four contracts, a buyer that neither accepts nor rejects within the window has accepted. The windows are 5 days for IREN, unit not stated; 7 business days for Cohere and Thinking Machines Lab; and redacted for Nebius.

5 of 10
Buyer may terminate for delay

In five contracts, filed text lets the buyer terminate for delivery or acceptance past a deadline. Only Cohere's contract shows such a deadline, 15 July 2027. The other five show no such right in the filed text, and each leaves part of its terms to unfiled order forms, service orders or a work letter.

Disclosures and gapsTwelve documents
  • Status, in a few cases. IREN's FY2026 10-K states that Horizon 1 "was delivered to, and accepted by, Microsoft in August 2026", and targets Horizons 2 to 4 for Q4 2026; its results release of 5 November places them in late-stage construction and moves its company-wide ARR target to January 2027, possibly February. No other filing read says a customer accepted anything. The others report delivery, operation or billing: Nebius reported in August that it had delivered all capacity tranches to Microsoft "to date"; Applied Digital's two CoreWeave buildings are operating; WhiteFiber began billing Nscale in Q3 2026 and reported in November that commissioning issues interrupted service and billing for the first 20 MW data hall, with about 35 MW of the contracted 40 MW now operational. SB Energy's Building 2 had not been delivered at 30 June 2026. A status line reports a passed gate or a completed delivery, without the evidence behind it.
  • The evidence behind a status. No filing read for this page contains a ready-for-test notice, a validation report, a test result or an acceptance notice.
  • Part of every test. In each of the twelve documents, some part of a test, deadline or remedy sits in an unfiled exhibit or is redacted. Each row of the map names the part.
  • The financing terms. Two financing arrangements also reach into acceptance gates. CoreWeave's $2.6B delayed-draw loan caps each draw at 70% of eligible GPU spend, and a replacement or additional customer contract qualifies only if, among other criteria, the customer has accepted any additional GPU clusters under it. IREN's financing of its Microsoft contract, about $3.6B in a delayed-draw loan and notes, conditions draws on a consultant's Level 4 data-hall certificate, delivery of any GPUs a draw pays for, and the customer's prepayment where paid; lowers the parent's minimum liquidity as Microsoft accepts each tranche; and requires prepayment, deferrable through a remarketing period, if Microsoft terminates a tranche for non-acceptance.
Reading the mapTerms
Acceptance; deemed acceptance
The customer's confirmation that delivered capacity meets the agreed criteria. A contract with deemed acceptance treats silence through the window, or production use, as acceptance.
Ready notice; RFS; commencement
A provider's notice that capacity is ready for testing or for service (RFS). In a lease, the commencement date starts the rent once the landlord meets the stated conditions; no customer test is involved.
MSA; order form; SOW
A master services agreement sets general terms. Order forms and statements of work (SOW) under it set quantities, prices and dates, and are often not filed.
Outside date; grace period
The last date for delivery or acceptance before the buyer may terminate.
DDTL
A delayed-draw term loan: committed up front, drawn in stages as conditions are met.
The contract decides acceptance
A contract's rule deeming silence or production use to be acceptance applies whatever any later record says. A record kept under SQCD-1, PSYCHE's open standard for delivery records (described under Method), shows each party's filings and their times; it does not replace a contract's acceptance rule.

MethodRead 24 October 2026 · status checked 5 November 2026

Selection. A selected set, not a census. The twelve documents are AI compute customer contracts and compute-backed financing agreements whose text, apart from redactions, is filed as an exhibit to an 8-K, 10-Q, 10-K, 20-F or S-1/A. They were found through EDGAR full-text search and the issuers' own filings in September and October 2026. Contracts known only from press releases are left out. Four further filed agreements were located and are not yet read clause by clause: two between Nebius and Meta, CoreWeave's 2025 order form with NVIDIA, and Applied Digital's Building 4 lease with CoreWeave.

Reading. Each exhibit was downloaded from sec.gov and read clause by clause on 24 October 2026; status lines were checked against each issuer's latest filings on 5 November 2026. Clause references point to the filed exhibit. The value usually comes from a different filing, named in each row.

Values. Each value is the full-term contract value as the cited filing states it: an approximate amount, a cap or an estimate. None is a remaining commitment or an unpaid balance, and the map does not restate values after later amendments. A customer contract counts toward $35.1B only where the filed text states a value and states that recurring fees or rent start on acceptance, a ready notice or commencement. A contract whose fee start sits in an unfiled document is shown and not counted. Financing arrangements are listed and never added. The exact arithmetic is under the chart, and the source ledger (CSV) gives each row's value source, clause source, classification and redactions. The source ledger is published under CC BY 4.0.

Disclosure. Nothing here is a view on any company's performance, and nothing here is investment advice. The author holds no position in any company named, and PSYCHE has no commercial relationship with any of them.

What PSYCHE is. PSYCHE keeps the record a delivery of contracted compute settles on. Each delivery window becomes one Case: both parties sign the terms before the window; each files its measurements under its own signature during the window; a verifier returns a result; the buyer, or a delegate it named, accepts or rejects within a review window, and silence ends the Case under a form both signed in advance. SQCD-1 is PSYCHE's open standard for that record, and anyone holding a record can recompute it. PSYCHE accepts nothing on anyone's behalf and holds no one's money.

Where fees and financing wait on acceptance, a lender, an insurer or an auditor needs a record of each passed gate. A Case is such a record: filed by both parties, with its terms fixed before the window, and recomputable by anyone who holds it. A Case's path through the network.
