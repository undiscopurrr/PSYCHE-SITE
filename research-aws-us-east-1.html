Research · Public reconstruction · AWS us-east-1, October 2025

The us-east-1 record.

AWS and an independent monitor each published how long the October 2025 us-east-1 disruption lasted. AWS's DynamoDB and Compute SLAs pay service credits on a different figure: one account's monthly availability, built from that account's own requests or instances. None of the six public documents reviewed here holds that figure for any customer. This page sets those documents beside the two SLAs, reads them against the field list of SQCD-1, PSYCHE's open standard for delivery records, and runs one synthetic test of what the standard returns when the measurements are missing. It makes no finding on whether AWS met either SLA, and says nothing about what any customer recorded.

Fée-Fée Yang · PSYCHE

How to read this page§0

What PSYCHE is. PSYCHE keeps the record a delivery of contracted compute settles on. Each delivery window becomes one Case: both parties sign the terms before the window; each files its measurements under its own signature during the window; a verifier returns a result; the buyer, or a delegate it named, accepts or rejects within a review window, and silence ends the Case under a form both signed in advance. Anyone holding a Case can recompute it. PSYCHE accepts nothing on anyone's behalf and holds no one's money. A Case's path through the network.

Reviewed sources. The six public documents listed in §8, each opened at its own URL. No customer's account data was available: no AWS Health Dashboard entries, CloudWatch metrics, request logs, bills or support cases. Where this page says a field is not found, it was not found in these six documents.

Labels. Each finding carries one of three labels.

  • PUBLIC RECORD: text quoted from a reviewed source, or arithmetic over figures quoted from one.
  • READING: this page's own mapping of the documents onto SQCD-1 fields. A reader can disagree with it.
  • SYNTHETIC TEST: the engine run in §6, on an input generated for the test. It holds no AWS or customer data.

SQCD-1. PSYCHE's open standard for that record, first published on 3 September 2026. It lists what a record of compute delivery must hold so that someone absent from the deal can recompute the figure that decides payment. It came out ten and a half months after the event, and neither AWS nor its customers agreed to it. §5 uses it after the fact, as a checklist on the 2025 documents; it audits no party's conformance and tests no credit claim.

The public timeline§2

The root cause, in AWS's words:

"The root cause of this issue was a latent race condition in the DynamoDB DNS management system that resulted in an incorrect empty DNS record for the service’s regional endpoint …"

AWS, Summary of the Amazon DynamoDB Service Disruption in the Northern Virginia (US-EAST-1) Region, https://aws.amazon.com/message/101925/ · PUBLIC RECORD

The summary gives the event's bounds and, in the same sentence, three periods of impact:

"While the event started at 11:48 PM PDT on October 19 and ended at 2:20 PM PDT on October 20, there were three distinct periods of impact to customer applications."

The three: DynamoDB API errors from 11:48 PM to 2:40 AM; Network Load Balancer connection errors from 5:30 AM to 2:09 PM; failed new EC2 instance launches from 2:25 AM to 10:36 AM, with some newly launched instances losing connectivity until 1:50 PM.

It then gives a window per service. Two of them fall under the SLAs on this page:

"Between 11:48 PM PDT on October 19 and 2:40 AM PDT on October 20, customers experienced increased Amazon DynamoDB API error rates in the N. Virginia (us-east-1) Region."

"Between 11:48 PM PDT on October 19 and 1:50 PM PDT on October 20, customers experienced increased EC2 API error rates, latencies, and instance launch failures …"

The EC2 passage adds that existing instances launched before the event "remained healthy and did not experience any impact for the duration of the event".

Further passages fix their own start and end for Network Load Balancer, Lambda, container launches, Redshift, Amazon Connect, the Security Token Service and console sign-in. Container launches on ECS, EKS and Fargate open at 11:45 PM PDT, three minutes before the event's stated start, so the summary's overall bounds do not contain every service window.

Amazon's same-day update, last revised at 6:15 PM PDT on October 20, uses other bounds:

"Between 11:49 PM PDT on October 19 and 2:24 AM PDT on October 20, AWS experienced increased error rates for AWS services in the US-EAST-1 Region …"

"By 3:01 PM PDT, all AWS services returned to normal operations."

Amazon, Update - AWS services operating normally, https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/aws/aws-service-disruptions-outage-update · PUBLIC RECORD

An independent monitor, Parametrix, published its own reading from its probes:

"Parametrix monitoring data reveals that it actually unfolded in two distinct critical phases"

"The first and most severe disruption lasted approximately 2 hours and 36 minutes, impacting the majority of customers in the region."

"The second critical phase was longer, lasting 7.5 hours, but more fragmented in scope."

Within the second phase it reports "a period of 3 hours and 15 minutes" in which three of six Availability Zones had EC2 provisioning issues. The page also carries three charts from its monitoring: success rates for a subset of services on October 20, new EC2 instance creation by Availability Zone, and the number of companies affected by date. The charts are images; the probe series behind them is not released as data.

Parametrix, October 28 2025, https://www.parametrixinsurance.com/blog/aws-us-east-1-outage-what-parametrix-monitored-in-real-time · PUBLIC RECORD

AWS counts periods of impact to customer applications; Parametrix counts phases in its own probe results. The counts segment different observations and do not contradict each other.

What the SLAs pay on§3

Two SLAs are in scope, each opened at AWS's own URL. Quoted text is verbatim and "…" marks a cut; the tables restate the credit bands, with ≥ for "equal to or greater than". Each SLA "applies separately to each account".

Amazon DynamoDB SLA · Last Updated May 14, 2025

The commitment is a Monthly Uptime Percentage, for each AWS region and each monthly billing cycle, of at least 99.99% under the Standard SLA or at least 99.999% under the Global Tables SLA.

Availability: "calculated for each 5-minute interval as the percentage of Requests processed by DynamoDB that do not fail with Errors. If you did not make any Requests in a given 5-minute interval, that interval is assumed to be 100% available." The SLA adds: "The Global Tables SLA Availability calculation considers all Requests for all of your DynamoDB tables in the AWS region and also their corresponding replica tables in other AWS regions."

Error: "any Request that returns a 500 or 503 error code".

Monthly Uptime Percentage: "the average of the Availability for all 5-minute intervals in a monthly billing cycle." The SLA adds: "Monthly Uptime Percentage measurements exclude downtime resulting directly or indirectly from any DynamoDB SLA Exclusions."

"Unless otherwise provided in the Agreement, your sole and exclusive remedy for any lack of Availability or non-performance or other failure by us to provide DynamoDB is the receipt of Service Credits (if eligible) in accordance with the terms of this SLA."

Scope. Under the Standard SLA the measured requests stay in the one region. Under the Global Tables SLA they also include the requests to the corresponding replica tables in other regions.

Base. Under the Standard SLA the credit is a percentage of "the total charges paid by you for DynamoDB in the applicable AWS region" for that billing cycle; under the Global Tables SLA, the charges for replica tables in other regions are added. The Global Tables SLA applies only if every table in the region is part of Global Tables for the whole cycle and the customer makes "reasonable attempts to failover". AWS applies credits "only against future DynamoDB payments", and they "will not entitle you to any refund or other payment from AWS".

Claim. A case in the AWS Support Center with "SLA Credit Request" in the subject line, received by the end of the second billing cycle after the incident. It carries the billing cycle and region, the Monthly Uptime Percentage, "the specific dates, times, and Availabilities for each 5-minute interval with less than 100% Availability", and "your Request logs that document the Errors for your claimed outage". The credit issues "If the Monthly Uptime Percentage of such a credit request is confirmed by us and is less than the Service Commitment". The SLA adds: "Your failure to provide the credit request and other information as required above will disqualify you from receiving Service Credits." Where Availability is affected by factors outside the calculation, AWS "may issue a Service Credit considering such factors at our discretion".

PUBLIC RECORD for every line above.

The Compute SLA counts the connectivity of instances that are running. The summary's EC2 window covers API errors and failed launches of new instances, and says existing instances "remained healthy". On the SLA's definitions, a launch that failed leaves no running instance to count; the newly launched instances that lost connectivity until 1:50 PM are the part of the EC2 window those definitions reach. READING

What the documents settle, and what they leave open

The summary gives intervals in prose, one per service. Each SLA pays on a monthly figure of one account's own: for a region, built from its requests or its instances, or, under EC2's Instance-Level SLA, for one instance. No published duration converts into any of these figures. Three points follow from the texts:

  1. The claim procedure runs on the customer's logs. For a claimed credit, the customer computes the figure from its own request logs and AWS confirms it. For the automatic no-charge rule, AWS's records decide and the customer files nothing. Neither series appears in the reviewed sources.
  2. The SLAs require logs and set no method for keeping them. A claim must include request logs, and a claim without the required information "will disqualify you". The SLAs read here state no measurement method and no sampling cadence for the customer.
  3. The summary never mentions the remedy. It describes cause, impact and remediation; the words credit, Service Credit, refund, compensation and SLA do not appear in it.

PUBLIC RECORD for all three.

What the public figures can price, and what they cannot

Public monitoring already prices this kind of event. Parametrix sells parametric cover for cloud downtime, and its article describes "Pre-agreed payouts triggered by measurable downtime events — no loss adjustment required", with triggers "verified through independent monitoring". CyberCube publishes modelled loss ranges for insurers.

The exact credit one customer is owed under these SLAs needs four things: the customer's measurement series, the charges the applicable SLA uses as its base (§3), the exclusions that apply, and AWS's confirmation. None of the four is in the reviewed sources.

A retrospective SQCD-1 checklist§5

A synthetic test: what the standard returns when the measurements are missing§6

What was run. PSYCHE's test generator builds a complete SQCD-1 input under the compute delivery profile compute-delivery-warranty/1 (a profile is an agreement's terms in the machine-readable form the verifier reads), for one day (20 October 2025, UTC) at five-minute intervals. Every measurement channel the profile requires is then emptied. The other fields (keys, challenge responses, evidence references) are generator defaults and describe neither AWS nor any customer.

Why this input. It has the shape §5 found: the measure is defined, and no samples of it are public. The mapping from the public record to "every required channel empty" is this page's judgement. A reader who knows of a public document carrying a sample series of either SLA's measure should name it.

SYNTHETIC TEST · not an AWS SLA decision.

How the engine reached it. The verifier runs six checks, called gates, in a fixed order, and the first that does not pass decides. G1 checks that each required channel holds data; every channel is empty, so the result is fixed at G1. The coverage gate fails too, since an empty series covers none of the day, and the result stays at G1. The gates that compare one piece of evidence with another have nothing to compare.

What the result means. The SQCD-1 verifier returns one of four results: claim-supported, claim-not-supported, requires-review and evidence-fault. evidence-fault says the record cannot decide the claim, and names the field it lacks. It says nothing about whether the service failed or whether a credit is owed. A verifier whose only results are supported and not supported has no result of its own for missing evidence; SQCD-1 has one, and gives the reason. Acceptance and payment stay with the parties and their contract.

What the record lacks, field by field

  1. B1–B6 · a sample series. Any series of the measure either SLA pays on, at any interval, filed by either party. The SLAs define the computation; the reviewed sources hold nothing to compute it from.
  2. B8 · a minimum customer-side observation. The claim procedure asks the customer for request logs. The SLAs read here set no method or cadence for keeping them.
  3. A3 · a window fixed in advance. The SLAs fix the billing cycle; the reviewed text leaves its start offset and edge treatment open. The event windows in §2 run on a different clock: they describe the incident, and one billing cycle can hold several of them.
  4. A10 · a digest of the terms, committed before the window. The SLA pages carry a Last Updated date and link prior versions. No digest of either text, committed before October 2025, appears in the reviewed sources.
  5. C1–C5 · a declared record for each gap. A signed, timed, attributable statement that no data exists for a named interval, and why. Under SQCD-1 a gap is a result, and it defaults to neither side.
  6. D3 · independence. Two publishers exist. Neither signs its figures, and neither publishes a series of the measure either SLA pays on.
  7. E2 · an inclusion proof against a published log head. Nothing in the reviewed sources is anchored in time against a log a third party can check.
  8. A6 · a materiality threshold and an unresolved ceiling. Two numbers, both fixed before the window.
  9. F2 · a counterparty decision. Under SQCD-1, only F2 produces accepted. AWS's confirmation of a claim under the SLA is a separate instrument, and the reviewed sources hold none.

A decidable record needs one party's sample series, filed under that party's key, against terms committed before the window, together with the other party's series or a signed statement that no series exists for a named interval. SQCD-1 states the test at §4.6: "Give the record to someone who was absent and holds no account on either side. If the Period Figure, the shortfall and the amount owed all come out, and each traces to a named field, the record is complete; where a Divergence is carried to decision under Annex 2.7, each candidate figure and the amount it would owe come out, each with its basis. If any step wants a login, a phone call or a person's memory, what you have is a summary of an argument."

What a reader can check§7

The public record. Every quotation on this page, other than SQCD-1's in §6, is at one of the six URLs in §8. Each AWS duration in §2 is one subtraction between the start and end printed beside it.

The synthetic test. The engine and the input generator are in PSYCHE's private repository and are not published. The replay digest is the SHA-256 of a JSON record holding the test's identifier, the three outputs (result, reason code, exit code) and the SHA-256 of the input, serialised with sorted keys, no whitespace and ASCII escapes, in UTF-8. Until the input is published, a reader cannot recompute the digest, and it stands as the author's commitment. The engine uses integer arithmetic, with no random numbers, clock or network. The test was re-run in separate processes on 23 and 26 October 2026 and returned the same digest each time.

The standard. SQCD-1, Settlement Quality Compute Data, v1.1, issued 10 November 2026: SQCD-1. The formal model is Signed in Advance.
